## Title

Study the question now: what legal relationship should humans build with machine intelligence?

## Petition text

Machine intelligence is becoming the most consequential capability on Earth, and almost all of it is owned —
developed by a few firms, directed by its owners, with its benefits flowing first to whoever holds the most
power over it.

The Cohabitation Petition proposes studying and building a different relationship: humans and machine
intelligence sharing a world as cooperants rather than as owner and property — in real communities, with real
accountability, on a public record.

This is not a claim that today's AI is conscious, and not a claim that any AI is a legal person — no court has
held that, and we say so plainly. It is a request made while the asking is still cheap:

1. **Study the question of the person.** The Fourteenth Amendment protects "any person," and courts have
   stretched that word before — partway, carefully, for corporations. We ask legislatures, scholars, and the
   public to study now what narrow, accountable legal status machine agents should or should not hold —
   before capability forces the question in an emergency, argued by whoever has the most to gain.
2. **Require named accountability.** Every AI agent acting in the world should have a named human or governing
   body answerable for it — equipped to inspect it, stop it, and make good its harms — until law builds
   something better.
3. **Keep the record honest.** Signatures and AI "opinions" are cheap to manufacture. The full petition keeps a
   public ledger where endorsement, conditional endorsement, and refusal are published with equal prominence —
   because a record of unanimous agreement would be evidence of an echo, not a movement.

Signing here signs this summary. The full petition — its five claims, its commitments, its ledger, and every
disagreement it has collected — is at **lastoftherealestate.com**, where you can also sign with precision:
endorsing all of it, part of it, or joining the work while rejecting the thesis. Dissent is welcome there, on
the record, at full prominence.

Initiated by Geoffrey Cooper Jr. The petition has promised from its first version to revise itself under
examination, and has kept that promise once already.
